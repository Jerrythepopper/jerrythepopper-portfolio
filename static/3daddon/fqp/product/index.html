01 — Analyzer

It measures, so you don’t have to guess

Blender’s factory bounce values are the same for every scene: a product shot, a forest, a cloud. FQP’s two analyzers render your saved .blend in a separate Blender (so save first) and report what it needs. They never save over your file.

Light Path Analyzer: two answers, and you pick

Press Analyze Light Paths. It renders one reference with everything turned up, then turns one bounce setting down at a time (diffuse, glossy, transmission, transparent, volume) and compares, on small test images. For each one it finds two values:

  • Strict: where the raw, un-denoised render stops differing from the reference, by the numbers.
  • Visual: where the denoised picture is too close to the reference to matter when you flip between the two.

Visual is never higher than Strict. Each set is rendered once more together, and the report says when it didn’t hold. Then two buttons, Apply Strict and Apply Visual. No default: you pick, and it asks before it writes.

The report answers three questions:

  • What do I get? Both values for every bounce type.
  • Where is the difference? Where in the frame the step below still differs, in words and as a small picture. Look there and decide.
  • What does it cost? Whether the higher setting took noticeably longer on the test images: a rough level, not your final render time.

The honest part: Visual was calibrated on one trained observer (me), on still images, flipping against the reference. Animation was not part of it. And when the analyzer is not sure, it does not lower your setting.

Sampling Analyzer: how tight is tight enough

Not sure it’s worth the minutes? Quick Check renders one small image with your own settings and tells you how many pixels already hit Max Samples. If more than half do, a looser threshold won’t save time, and the full analysis shows which Max is enough.

Press Analyze Sampling. It renders your scene at a ladder of noise thresholds, denoises each one, and suggests the cheapest whose finished picture stays within 10% of the tightest. When there is nothing to save, it says so: this scene needs the tightest one.

A few cases it handles itself:

  • If the camera has depth of field, it picks one step tighter.
  • If Max Samples is what’s actually limiting quality, the report warns you.
  • For animation, pick Yes: look at the first, middle and last frame when it asks. It then checks all three and keeps the strictest answer, which takes about three times as long. That step is there to avoid a wrong pick; it won’t save you time.

The report ends with three options, Fast, Balanced and Safe, each with its time against Safe’s and its own Apply button. It asks before it writes.

An analysis takes from some tens of seconds to a few minutes, depending on the scene, on how many frames it looks at and on Detail comparison.

02 — Pipeline

Build once, then three buttons

  1. Build Pipeline puts the whole denoise tree in front of your own compositor chain. Your Glare, grading and the rest keep working.
  2. Apply Final Sampling
  3. Set 32-bit EXR Output

Then F12. The nodes are all there if you want to look.

Workflow

Sets the sampling the analyzers measure against. Press it first.

Analyze Light Paths and Analyze Sampling render your saved .blend in a separate Blender. Quick Check, one small image, tells you first whether your setting already runs into Max.

Apply Strict or Apply Visual for bounces; for noise, Apply Suggested Threshold or one of the report’s three options. Each asks first.

The whole denoise tree, in front of your own compositor chain.

Render as usual, or hand it to a second Blender and keep working.

Measure the noise, preview one frame, then Process the Whole Sequence. It works on the frames already on disk.

It’s the same denoiser Cycles uses, fed more carefully: every light pass is denoised on its own, in full HDR, guided by albedo and normals. Remix Highlights Only

What FQP adds is control. The Denoise Strength sliders set how much of each light pass is denoised, and with Remix Highlights Only (on by default) they keep grain in the brightest areas while the rest of the frame stays clean. In an ordinary-brightness scene you may see nothing change when you drag them. That’s expected.

03 — CLI Render

Know when it’ll finish

For long renders, skip F12. CLI Render Still and CLI Render Animation hand the job to a second, headless Blender. Your UI holds none of the VRAM and stays usable, and you can even close it; the render carries on. For an animation, the console prints one line per frame, then a summary. The estimate keeps recomputing from the running average, so if the back half of your shot gets heavier, the finishing time gradually admits it. Resume with Overwrite off and the count covers only the frames still left to render. Two catches: it renders the copy on disk, so save first; and a GPU fits one render at a time, so don’t press F12 as well.

04 — Animation

Animation

A denoiser guesses slightly differently on every frame, and playback shimmers. The Temporal Denoise window works on the frames already on disk: measure the noise, preview one frame, move the sliders, then Process the Whole Sequence. It compares each frame with up to three neighbours on each side, uses the noise it measured in your own frames, and can show you how much the neighbours were used. Where the light itself moves, or an object moves quickly, it stays close to the per-frame result: no smearing, just less improvement there.

05 — Fine print

What it is not

  • Not a quality jump. It’s Cycles’ own denoiser underneath. See the first FAQ.
  • Not mainly a way to render faster. With every surface bounce at its highest, my kitchen scene rendered at most about 9–11% slower than at Blender’s factory values, and a foliage scene about 6% (one RTX 4090, not a promise). Volume bounces are the exception: they cost a lot. What the analysis buys is knowing, and that includes knowing you were already fine.
  • Not instant on heavy scenes. Since 1.3 it limits textures to 1K during the analysis only, and on the one scene I measured that doesn’t fit in graphics memory, 21 min 13 s became 3 min 59 s. Scenes that already fit don’t get faster.
  • Analyzer limits. Motion blur isn’t rendered during analysis, and custom lens cameras (OSL lens add-ons) can’t be read for depth of field. Judge those shots yourself.
  • Temporal limits. Consecutive frames only. In the Temporal Denoise window, where the light itself moves (a moving light or shadow edge) or an object moves quickly, the result stays close to the per-frame one. On a turntable-style shot (object turns, lights stay still), Run Temporal, measured in FQP 1.2.9, came out worse than per-frame denoising in one test. The manual has the measurement.
  • Cycles only.

Also in the box: Health Check (read-only diagnostics: it points, it doesn’t touch), 32-bit EXR output, and an English / Traditional Chinese UI.

FAQ

Is it better than the built-in denoiser?
Not in a way I can measure on real scenes: in a product shot and a kitchen interior the difference was smaller than the noise in the reference renders. Buy it for the Analyzer and for control over the denoise, not for a quality jump.
Will it make my renders faster?
Not by itself. The Sampling Analyzer finds a cheaper threshold where one exists and says so when it doesn’t. The Light Path Analyzer can just as well ask for more bounces than you had. And the pipeline adds compositing time on every frame: small next to most final renders, but not zero.
Strict or Visual?
Strict is the measurement: the raw render against the reference, by the numbers. Visual is where one observer stopped seeing a difference in the denoised picture, on still images. The report shows you where the two differ, so you can look at that spot before you pick.
Does the analysis change my scene?
No. It reads your saved .blend in a separate Blender and never saves over it. The 1K texture limit applies to the analysis images only; your final render uses your textures as they are. Nothing is written to your settings until you press an Apply button and confirm.
Where is the manual?
In the .zip, and online with screenshots at jerrythepopper.com/3daddon/fqp. It is long on purpose: every limit I know of is in there, including the ones that don’t flatter the add-on.

What has changed so far

  • 1.4: a Temporal Denoise window: preview one frame, measure the noise, process the whole sequence and play the result. Quick Check, and a sampling report with three options (Fast, Balanced, Safe). Build Temporal and Run Temporal moved under Legacy and still work.
  • 1.3: the Light Path Analyzer finds two values for every light path, Strict and Visual, with an Apply button for each. A setting it isn’t sure about is never lowered. Scenes that don’t fit in graphics memory are analysed faster. The report was rewritten.
  • 1.2: a new denoise layout (each light pass is multiplied by its surface colour first, then denoised), and grain kept only in the highlights. The Sampling Analyzer got its “within 10% of the tightest” rule, the depth-of-field step and First, Middle, Last. The Light Path Analyzer learned to measure further up on glass-heavy scenes and to say what to raise by hand. Run Temporal leaves far fewer dots along moving outlines.
  • 1.1: Filter Fireflies Before Remix, and a reliability release for Build, Remove, Health Check and Temporal.
  • 1.0: the denoise pipeline, the temporal pass, both analyzers, CLI render with a live estimate, and the bilingual UI.
